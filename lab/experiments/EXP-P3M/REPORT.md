## Result and eight-item implementation

Implemented an offline-qualified native-feed **NS P3 entry point**, `lab/bench/p3-driver.mjs`, with provider instructions in this directory's `README.md`. This is builder evidence for the measurement harness, not independent verification, native lane acceptance or NS.P3 closure. The original `driver.ts` remains its A0 journal control; its old ACK-derived gap checks and constant phase names do not become native P3 evidence.

1. **Feed route:** the consumer selects FD01's public `brokerChangeFeed`, `xtdbChangeFeed` or explicit Datomic adapter. No call to `transactions()` and no automatic journal fallback. The fake Q source exercises the actual broker adapter and FD01 host store.
2. **Publication:** FD01's host/boot-bound clock, producer SQLite writer/reader and per-position immutable publication records. `openSourceRecorder` provides synchronous owner hooks for broker watermark publication, X COMMIT SYNC return (retaining `systemTime`) and D TS transact result. Final audit checks publication identity against source transaction/operation identity, the lane event kind, broker watermark instant or X's retained engine time. Native owners must invoke these at the actual event; the fixture cannot prove that placement on a real engine. Engine time is never subtracted from host time. Missing records fail before the page's consumer progress commits.
3. **Final prefix:** provider `finalPrefix()` enumerates source records independently of delivery/ACKs. FD01 `auditFeed` byte-compares the delivered prefix, including abort/epoch/seal resolutions, and verifies order/identity/command accounting and terminal cursor. Every committed position must have a producer instant and exactly one sample. ACK reconciliation follows that audit. The fixture's lost-ACK `cmd-2` stays in coverage; a dropped delivery and phantom ACK are rejected.
4. **Isolation:** a dedicated consumer worker owns SQLite durability and feed polling. Four other workers execute reader clients. Main thread owns source writer and orchestration. Reader errors, early exits or failure to reach the final prefix refuse the receipt; all worker handles are terminated on cleanup.
5. **Reader execution:** default 256 separately observed closed-loop clients across four reader workers; 0 through 1024 can be requested for diagnostics/stress. Readers perform their first pull before readiness and another after the measuring window; first/last completed times, completed counts and errors are checked. There is no artificial think time. The provider must open the named lane composition and supply TARGETS P1 subject/basis mixes. The offline provider uses immutable fake-source snapshots; it proves harness concurrency, not native P1 correctness, load fidelity or goodput.
6. **Pooling:** nearest-rank percentiles over every measured-window publication, bursts included, plus separate steady/burst summaries. Exact raw millisecond samples avoid histogram rounding around 250 ms. Publication outside the window remains prefix-accounted but excluded from that window's lag distribution. Empty measured windows are refused.
7. **Labels:** receipt `readerLevel` is the observed unique loop count covering the measured window, checked against the requested count. Zero-reader diagnostic reports zero. Removing a reader or claiming L256 for zero readers is rejected.
8. **Binding:** unique attempt UUID, attempt-bound source identity, lockfile, provider and declared workload/dependency inputs, complete core/host/testkit dist trees, P3 runner bytes, final source-prefix bytes, consumer-store bytes/pragmas, producer-record database, raw observations, feed route, reader composition and slot identity. Offline slot is null. Live invocation requires slot metadata and returns MEASURED, never an admission/promotion verdict; admission and owner verification belong to the calling engine gate. Source/build/input hashes are checked again at run end.

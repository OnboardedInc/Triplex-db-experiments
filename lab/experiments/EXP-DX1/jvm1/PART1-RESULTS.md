- **Present.** The earlier 33 ms anchor at 23.5/s was too optimistic. Native await alone reached 71 ms p99. At 39.4/s, scheduled-to-sender and native-await p99 reached 273 and 255 ms. Sparse arrivals increased the median in every arm even with small sender backlog. There is no isolated idle timer or shrinking-pool finding.
- **32 MiB.** Solo 23.5/s p99 nearly doubled, from 72 to 139 ms. Native await rose from 71 to 135 ms while sender-to-native p99 stayed at 10–11 ms. The bundle also changed placement, heap and compilation. Compare 32 to 128 MiB to isolate the threshold. Transactor GC events over whole arms were 515 on present, 1,174 on 32 MiB and 1,140 on 128 MiB. GC events alone cannot attribute a save to an index job.
- **128 MiB.** At 235 and 394 offered/s, it delivered 188.5 and 186.4 saves/s versus 148.8 and 163.5 at 32 MiB. The 235/s backlog fell from 5,515 to 2,332 and drain beyond the window from 34.8 to 14.8 s. Quiet p99 fell from 1,203 to 981 ms against nine at 23.5/s and from 1,072 to 956 ms at 39.4/s. It still missed the solo forecast and paid a 43 ms solo penalty at 39.4/s against 32 MiB. Less frequent indexing is a plausible explanation for the busy improvement. The retained collector summaries lack a matched per-job duration and overlap census.
- **Compiler scaling 1.** On the 32 MiB candidate, solo 39.4/s ACK p99 rose from 200 to 627 ms. Scheduled-to-sender p99 rose from 2 to 430 ms, and native await from 199 to 325 ms. The timing supports accumulated queue delay. It does not distinguish compilation from other upstream stalls. The smaller quiet ratios also reflect a slower solo denominator. Every arm missed the 1.25× quiet bar with either of the two higher-rate neighbour loads.

# After the first aging ladder

The source-bound ladder completed on the held volume. The correct reading is 59, 44 and 30 completed and adopted index jobs, with sustained rates of 15,242.78, 16,330.62 and 18,081.59 indexed records/s. The original certificate stays intact. The corrected checker requires its exact digest, outer custody inventory, original source and attempt, all original artifacts, the matching transactor PID and one physical database identity across the three arms. On the server it also checks the same native catalog ID, held checkpoint and live cut before the first continuation write.

## What limited throughput

CPU usage was 2.66–2.71 of three cores. Completed index job durations sum to 688, 749 and 762 seconds in 828–836 second windows. These are about 83%, 90% and 91% of each arm. Apply summed to 724, 799 and 814 seconds (44, 56 and 54 microseconds per indexed record). The per-transaction observations overlap and cannot be subtracted from wall time. WAL sync means were 0.170, 0.266 and 0.296 ms. The WAL sync times summed to 219, 188 and 199 seconds, including indexing. There were no back-pressure alarms, and the memory index stayed under 458 MiB against a 2 GiB cap. Larger groups cut physical transaction counts from 818,218 to 70,275 and 25,112, but the work to apply and index each record remained. Saturated CPU and concurrent index work are the strongest supported bottleneck. The trace leaves I/O stalls inside individual jobs possible.

The old arm changed five settings and available CPU together. The evidence cannot identify the largest individual effect among the three-core allowance, object cache, heap, index threshold and parallelism. WAL stayed durable and was fast in the new arms. Large memory-index caps did not come into play during the ladder. The new continuation spends more CPU on the proven solo layout, caps indexing at four workers on six physical cores, and raises the threshold from 128 MiB to 256 MiB to reduce frequent segment replacement. It keeps the 24 GiB heap, 16 GiB object cache, 2 GiB memory-index maximum and write concurrency of four. Pin PostgreSQL separately. Keep synchronous commit, fsync and full-page writes on. ## Before-run predictions and stop rules

Use solo K at depth 256. The 24-member arm was faster by sustained indexed count but added 0.91 GB per million against solo's 0.71 and changed index geometry. This continuation retains the already-written grouped spans. Predict 20–30k indexed records/s during writing and **18–26k/s including collection, vacuum and index adoption**. From 477,456,017 that implies **5.58–8.06 loading hours**, or **$6.08–$8.79** remaining. Including the completed 44m47s ladder gives **6.33–8.81 hours and $6.90–$9.60**. Start with 20 minutes of writing, then measure the combined record advance through the first maintenance cycle. Stop if the combined rate falls below 9,900/s or no longer fits 16 loading hours including the ladder. Keep sampling across later cycles. The raw events still distinguish active write throughput and index work from maintenance time. A rate near the old ladder despite six busy cores and a settled index would falsify the expected scaling. A rate near the ladder with spare CPU redirects attention to index/SQL waits or the loader.

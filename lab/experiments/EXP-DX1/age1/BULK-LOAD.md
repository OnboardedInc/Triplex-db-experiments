# Recovering the one-billion run

## What the original run measured

The SCALE1 archive SHA-256 is `504214da1a298e4b310eecb079503effe257603314a0d1310f3aa05d56d04b17`. Its 100,000 saves added 2,000,000 native log records in 378.514 s, or 264.19 saves/s and 5,283.82 records/s. At depth 256 the loader spent 347.477 s in `d/transact-async` submission, 17.141 s encoding, 6.623 s verifying replies, 4.707 s awaiting the head future and 2.405 s generating the tape. Submission includes waiting for the peer and transactor pipeline to accept work. The clock stops at the peer API, so it cannot allocate the wait inside the transactor.

The rung JVM lived 382.9 s. Over its life the transactor logged 32.88 s of GC pauses and 34.17 s in total safepoints, including GC. This bounds the direct pause share of the timed segment at about 9%. The loader logged 1.51 s total safepoints. Adoption and inspection were outside that interval. `inspect.log` records all three SQL durability switches as on. `adopt.log` finished its explicitly requested seed index before the calibration. The server's `tx.log` contains launch and ready messages. Discovery established the scope, with no storage wait or index telemetry. The evidence leaves transaction apply, cache misses, durable SQL/WAL writes, and index back-pressure unresolved. A single synchronized trace across two natural indexing cycles, with sampled native `tx/process` apply/segment-I/O, index job events, memory-index alarms and `pg_stat_wal` deltas with timing enabled, distinguishes those cases.

The 24k forecast extrapolated a 20.1k/s small, contended Mac high point upward by 19%. It required 1,200 solo saves/s on a 430M-record database. The server delivered 264. The growth in native transaction capacity that the forecast assumed never materialized. The full-size writer comparison gives 202–362 saves/s with different profiles and a different writer; the BM1 small arm improved 138.6 to 188.5 saves/s at 128 MiB. Those comparisons motivate the tuned solo control and a wider batch; they do not allocate the missing time. [Original calibration](../scale1/V01-CAMPAIGN.md#age1-measured-calibration)

## Load format and predicted calibration

Use the same held private PostgreSQL volume and the verified 100,000-save prefix at native t 34,766,053, logical head 187,002. The runner checks the original archive's checkpoint and population hashes and the live cut, preserves their tape and clocks, adds only the W1 group schema, and continues on that copy. Keep the source seed pristine. Use the same tuned transactor for all three arms, one loader, and the same tape, rotating owners and width distribution. Stop at two or more complete, naturally triggered and adopted indexes per *eligible* arm. The normal arm spans 14 minutes of writing. A bounded drain and exact index census follow the window and do not contribute to its index count. The rate used for admission is the lower of the full-window rate and the final steady windows, using unique indexed records after checking the settled count. Fewer than two in-window jobs give no sustained estimate. A larger threshold gets no exemption. Expected costs include ladder loading time, and the aging clock allows at most 16 hours combined at $1.09/hour. The target is 24k/s, about 6.58 loading hours from the held cut before the cost of experimenting; 9.9k/s just fits in 15.94 h if the average across the entire prefix continuation holds.

| Arm | Proposed native size and depth | Indexed records/s prediction | What would disconfirm it |
| --- | --- | ---: | --- |
| Tuned solo | 1 save × 256 outstanding | 7,000–9,500 | Above 9,500 through two indexes suggests settings or storage were more limiting than estimated. Below 7,000 despite low WAL/lookup stalls and no alarms weakens the settings explanation. |
| Tuned grouped | 8 distinct saves × 16 | 11,000–19,000 | A sustained rate close to solo with the submit queue full and a similar WAL/index profile means per-save apply, encoding or index work dominates the physical transaction overhead. |
| Tuned grouped | 24 distinct saves × 16 | 16,000–28,000 | At or below arm 8 despite high in-flight utilization suggests intra-transaction apply/index costs or shared storage congestion. Below 24k misses the fast target even if it meets the budget. |

The predictions are priors. They discount the increase in saves per native commit for larger apply batches, more index work and loader verification. If telemetry shows a starved transactor, raise depth through non-overlapping owners with the same runner before considering a second loader. The prior solo arm already spent 92% of its time inside submit with 256 outstanding. A second loader adds contention and independent ordering/recovery requirements and is outside this ladder.

**Datomic Pro 1.0.7745.** Set 24 GiB heap and three processors, with `object-cache-max=16g`, `memory-index-threshold=128m`, `memory-index-max=2g`, `index-parallelism=2`, and `write-concurrency=4` (default storage write concurrency, held fixed). These are the transactor keys from the shipped SQL template, with batching and pipelining from Cognitect's import guidance. The lower 128 MiB trigger leaves room to see jobs in the calibration. The 2 GiB cap permits indexing through longer merges. The heap leaves ~6 GiB beyond both caps. Two index workers leave CPU for apply, with no assumption that all indexing parallelizes. Keep PostgreSQL `fsync`, `synchronous_commit` and `full_page_writes` on. Enable `track_wal_io_timing` for observation. Keep PostgreSQL/WAL placement constant across the ladder. No storage GC, cache priming, or Datomic modifications. [Datomic import and sizing guidance](https://docs.datomic.com/operation/capacity.html#data-imports) · [Background indexing](https://docs.datomic.com/indexes/background-indexing.html) · [Monitoring](https://docs.datomic.com/operation/monitoring.html) · [1.0.7745 release history](https://docs.datomic.com/changes/pro.html)

**Grouped format.** Each accepted save keeps its ordered identity, operation, clock, receipt, asserted and retracted field rows, and exact journal bytes. One physical `t` and `:db/txInstant` cover up to 8 or 24 members. The final member occupies the physical transaction entity and carries the final position/max clock; each interior member gets its own entity and logical position/clock. W1 also adds member/physical/touched refs, position keys and per-row retirement markers. Explicit and implicit prior-revision retractions appear together in the raw log. The indexed history census counts unique datoms; one physical log entry can contain many saves. Logical pins inside a group use the group's overlay. Raw native t therefore cannot represent the interior cuts directly. The altered ref and key mix, leaf packing, journal locality and total number of saves change with grouping. This is suitable for asking how a one-billion-native-record database with deep history behaves. It cannot isolate history size from physical journal layout or establish throughput for one physical transaction per historical save. Save latency measurements still use the solo product path. Label an aged outcome with the selected arm and all calibration spans, including the solo prefix and mixed group sizes.

## Driver setup and commands

Use the same SCALE1 held-volume custodian as the original run. Start the held working volume in place, and keep the old archive, immutable SCALE1 image and fresh baseline outside it. Point a **new** `output` directory at a sibling of the old `rung` directory, with the original same-parent `age1-<digest>.edn` and `.population.edn` in place. Do not copy the old admission into the new output. Keep the previous [config shape and private runtime environment](SERVER-RUNG.md#invocation). Put the new clean source revision in `source`, set `fromRecords=431778659`, `ratePrior=24000`, and supply the admitted transactor's real PID, properties digest and current Java inventory. Keep the SQL and copy guards and reserve real capacity for the full rung and its native copies.

Apply the five properties above through the current driver's transactor launcher. Its four-key `expectedMeasureSettings.properties` stays four keys, and the runner independently reads `write-concurrency`. Use `-Xmx24g -XX:ActiveProcessorCount=3` and the admitted three-core affinity. To the transactor JVM, append `-Dlogback.configurationFile=<absolute path to bulk-logback.xml>` and set `AGE_TX_EVENT_LOG` to an absolute path on reserved evidence storage. Set `bulkEventLog` in the runner JSON to this exact path. Use a fresh log file for the restarted JVM; keep its raw bytes with the run. The runner checks that the admitted PID has the exact appender bytes and an open file descriptor to this inode. Enable WAL timing in PostgreSQL and retain the original JDBC durability. In the SCALE1 wrapper, set its discovery cut to the actual last native logical head (initially 187,002) and invoke the following subcommands in place of the original solo entry. On a subsequent resume derive the head from the mutable checkpoint plus its admitted pending window. No process should make a new application write or index request during calibration.

```sh
node lab/experiments/EXP-DX1/age1/server.mjs ladder "$AGE_RUNG_CONFIG"
node lab/experiments/EXP-DX1/age1/server.mjs load "$AGE_RUNG_CONFIG"
node lab/experiments/EXP-DX1/age1/server.mjs measure-loaded "$AGE_MEASURE_CONFIG"
```

A nonzero ladder exit means stop. Its bound artifact reports every arm and, when available, the qualifying measured rate. The full load rejects rates under 9,900 and checks fit against the remaining loading clock, recalculating from 20-minute rolling samples after it starts. It verifies one physical group at a time, preserves a bounded pending interval before sending, and reconciles a stopped suffix against the exact native log before sending more. Retain the output directory and both state files through all stages. The final load stops only after the indexed count has been checked at or above 1B, with bounded overshoot; native raw transaction-log records alone do not qualify.

For measurements, use the same output and target but an independently selected measurement profile with a fresh transactor and updated process read-back. Run the existing matched solo save windows, index job overlap, fresh JVM point reads, directory census and verified native backup/restore. There is no prep read or cache warmup added by the bulk loader. The scorecard still consumes the same version-1 `aged-scorecard.json` and the stopped PostgreSQL directory at the sealed cut from the custodian. The current SCALE1 wrapper also exports a hash-pinned `aged.pgdump` for the pinned `--aged-bundle` driver. Retain both as before; establish custody by stopping application writers and PostgreSQL at the exact sealed cut. Publish the calibration and bulk-load evidence next to that handoff. The JSON's `journal` and `layout` identify the solo tail the verifier reads. This bundle does not describe the historical grouping; `bulk-loaded.json` and the sealed `agingEvidence` digest carry that provenance.

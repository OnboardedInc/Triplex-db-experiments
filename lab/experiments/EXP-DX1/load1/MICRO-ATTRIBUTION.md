Shares use the faster control and describe possible or guaranteed overlap, with a 0.29–0.68 ms client-to-guest clock interval and 1 ms resolution at each GC endpoint. Shares can overlap and are unaudited as causal allocations. Grouped commits omit the absolute dispatch timestamp. The two endpoints of the request bound the estimate of its ready interval. The middle of the range can shift slightly if the enqueue handoff stalls. The native implementation gives reads no turn in the write lane. Read records contain offer and completion only, so even a pause overlap cannot locate the exact read phase. A zero means no direct event overlapped the p99 operation, with other causes unresolved.

## Preregistered server comparison

Measure one-frame admission at 32 facts with **110 tokens/s fixed** and with **110 tokens/s maximum and live-latency feedback**, keeping the same gateway, transactor and PostgreSQL. Test feedback first after the leading control. The earlier bulk hold medians were 1.86–2.63 ms, p99s 11.23–12.26 ms, and bulk holds occupied 27–38% of the windows. More requests in flight can overlap idle time and add allocation and peer work. It cannot take a live save past the long already-submitted hold. Separation of the import gateway may reduce allocation on the live gateway, but independent peers would need common writer admission and would leave the major observed hold intact. Index rescheduling has no direct overlap at these p99 points. The feedback experiment targets both bulk-hold exposure and allocation spikes without changing the write order.

Use one token bucket with no backlog and the existing maximum of one frame outstanding. Every eight seconds compare the last 16 seconds of each account's offered-to-complete save/read latency to the first control. Penalize overdue or missed offers. At a ratio above 1.35, reduce the next token rate by 20%, to a floor of 32/s. After a healthy interval below 1.20, add 8 tokens/s, capped at 110/s. The late control independently prices baseline drift.

These estimates start from 3,983 queryable facts/s at the historical 130/s offer ceiling. At least 93.75 complete, queryable frames/s are required for 3,000 facts/s. The predicted fixed cap leaves about 14% of tokens for stalls or unfinished work. Feedback is expected to average 64–92 granted frames/s, with a smaller gap between ready and dispatched saves and fewer gateway pause overlaps. I expect the two arms to miss at least one bar. Feedback is contradicted if it materially reduces the offered rate and bulk holds but leaves the quiet save and read p99s within 10% of the fixed arm, or if a verified arm clears both bars against both bookends. The experiment leaves storage attribution open until request-correlated SQL waits or native writer events exist.

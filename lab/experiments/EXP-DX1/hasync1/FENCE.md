# Whole-machine fencing and renewed protection

With two data machines, the surviving copy contains the saves already confirmed before the crash. A separate decision governs the next save.

| Policy | What the customer experiences | Impact on the 60-second write target |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Accept saves on the survivor and raise an alarm | Work can resume while a replacement copies data. A second machine loss can erase saves accepted during that window. | Plausible after a verified fence and promotion. Time to protect new saves is measured separately. |
| Wait for two durable copies | Reads stay available at verified cuts. Saves receive a refusal until there is a second copy. A client keeps the same command ID. | The time to obtain and copy to a replacement is part of the outage. There is no finite bound without an available destination. |
| Run three full data copies | The surviving pair can acknowledge saves with two copies. A management witness alone holds no data. | Plausible in 18–40 s at 16M and 20–50 s at 160M with a working fence, bounded WAL replay and ready peers. The three-copy workflow remains a design. |

With three copies, require a remote flush before reply. If PostgreSQL uses `ANY 1 (b,c)`, compare the two survivors' durable contiguous WAL prefixes on the registered source timeline, select the most advanced eligible copy, fence the original, and check that the other survivor follows the winner and is synchronous before opening ingress. If either candidate's prefix is unavailable, stop unless an independently durable receipt floor establishes completeness. An arbitrary standby can be behind an acknowledged commit. PostgreSQL supplies streaming replication, synchronous acknowledgment, promotion and rewind. [PostgreSQL synchronous replication](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/17/warm-standby.html#SYNCHRONOUS-REPLICATION)

## Independent boot and promotion authority

- Put management on its own power and network path. Enroll the old machine's power channels, storage and writer identities out of band. The old host has no credentials to turn on those channels or change the write interlock. A management device must hold all feeds off until a separate authenticated release, including across electrical loss and a management reboot. If using storage fencing instead, the storage controller must revoke the old machine across all write paths and persist that revocation.
- On detection, close write ingress and verify the survivor's PostgreSQL cluster, timeline and durable prefix. The management authority durably records revocation for the exact owner and generation, operates the interlock, reads it back independently, and only then returns a challenge-bound ticket. No health timeout authorizes promotion. No machine clock, elapsed lease or cached “off” response authorizes a boot. If the interlock or independent read-back is unavailable, keep writes closed.
- Run one serialized authority service with its journal on management-owned durable storage. The provisioned adapter must authenticate the caller and translate `fence` and `observe` into physical device operations. `FenceAuthority` is the persistent protocol core and `powerFence` is the supervisor adapter. Provision the production adapter against the real device and exercise loss of each power feed, the management link, and power to the controller before relying on it.
- For two data hosts, preassign the only candidate allowed to promote in each generation. With multiple eligible candidates, persist the selected writer through the management quorum before granting write authority. A receipt that fences the old host cannot authorize multiple new writers.
- A repaired host may return as a standby only after its disk is checked and rewound onto the winning timeline or replaced with a new verified backup. The old writer remains latched. Re-arm the independent authority for the new writer and a greater registered generation before relying on a second promotion.

The Mac checks the protocol with a restarted authority and opposite wall-clock offsets of 24 hours. The database drill keeps the old PostgreSQL disk, checks its original system ID in a fresh container, and checks that the old startup latch refuses it. That simulates a returned host disk. Real switched power and controller isolation require the paired servers and management hardware. ## Replacing a lost copy

A physical copy streams while the promoted primary runs. Catch up, require the new copy's synchronous flush, and read a fresh protected receipt. At an assumed effective 75–150 MB/s and estimated physical footprints of 6–12 GB and 60–120 GB, budget about 45–180 seconds at 16M and 7–27 minutes at 160M. These estimates include roughly 5–20 seconds for startup and bounded final replay. Physical size grows with retained edit history, WAL and the SQL cluster's other schemas. Rewind can reuse the original disk if its common timeline and WAL survive. It copies changed blocks and files and replays the fork, so its clock is separate from full-copy time.

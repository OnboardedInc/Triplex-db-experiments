# Separating bytes, journal work and the native guard

**North star.** P2 low-latency durable saves with the P3 stored journal. [Scope](../../../SCOPE.md) · [Priorities](../../../NORTH-STAR.md) · [Targets](../../../TARGETS.md)

The Part 1 ratios of 4.40× and 6.19× compare whole paths with a shallow tape, an older W1 control and unequal obligations. The floor fits are for raw authored datoms, warm indexed seeks and payload bytes. They cannot identify what fraction of that historical speedup came from any one feature.

At depth 64 the measured arms averaged 7.5 members per transaction. The incumbent added about 26 datoms and 4.34 KiB per member. At the floor slopes that increment accounts for about 0.056 ms/member of native apply and 0.42 ms/member of durable peer time, with lookup count and fixed cost held constant. The measured native apply gap was 0.85–0.97 ms/member. This calculation leaves variant-specific validation, row/index shape, cache misses and group behavior unresolved. The larger incumbent frames also extend beyond the 16 KiB floor range. [Part 1 tape](MEASUREMENTS.md) · [Floor](FLOOR.md)

The fixed-group probe runs 24 groups of eight per arm, reversing the arm order on the second tape block. One strict incumbent scope and one compact account use the same 1–3 replacements on distinct 160-fact subjects. The corrected gateway source is staged from WFIX1. Its installed optimized predecessor function and active small-row, prepared-reuse, group and clock settings are read back before timing. The incumbent calls the real row preparation, speculative apply and materialization functions, then waits on the same whole-basis guarded native commit used in its writer. This fixes group size and separates peer work from durable acceptance. Queue scheduling and HTTP are measured separately or omitted from this diagnostic.

For compact, the plain and stored-journal arms share the same root-local guard. The direct arm precomputes those same compact operations from the identical isolated view and submits them as raw native data. It prices native materialization with a fixed predecessor and belongs only in the diagnostic. Comparing append envelopes with and without journal rows controls layout and receipt work at that boundary. Comparing corrected raw append with the journal arm prices different physical representations under agreed logical replacements, with different business query surfaces. A separately checked native timing record joins every group to its exact writer, t and committed datoms.

WFIX1's normal grouped append runs the legacy transaction function once per member speculatively. It enforces old assertion IDs, predecessor identity and live retirements, and then a whole-database basis guard applies materialized rows. PREP1 moved preparation ahead and rechecked the exact prior rows, scope flags, journal absence and historical IDs in the native guard. Its report found 85.8% of grouped peer turns in speculative apply and materialization and, in a different prepared control, a native apply median of 89.0/89.8 ms per full group. Compact verifies the assigned command, account and owner heads, plus protected generation/version claim when applicable. Fresh assertion IDs come from the serialized account position and ordinal. The peer validates all changes against the exact owner head, and the native head check means those same input intervals still hold. The guard does not implement general Triplex graph constraints or arbitrary imported identity reuse. [PREP1 measured controls](https://github.com/OnboardedInc/Triplex-db-experiments/blob/0bcc9ec55/lab/experiments/EXP-DX1/prep1/REPORT.md)

The existing layout can retain every queryable row while eliminating redundant encoding, sharing staged values, and replacing peer speculation where the new authoritative checks prove the same invariants. Moving checks into Datomic only helps when their total serialized work falls. It can avoid historical ID scans for a separately proved non-reusable ID range and preserve an exact fallback for other IDs. Q107-S requires each ordinary change and changed entity. Packing those plain facts away or suppressing their membership changes the surface. Removing broad invariant checks requires a proof of narrower scopes for the affected command types.

The packed and stored-journal variants were **3.75× and 2.68×** faster at the group median. Storing the compact journal raised the group median by **3.61 ms**. The corrected incumbent's speculative apply was **35.4% of the full group time** and **61.0% of peer work** at the median of per-group shares. Evaluation plus materialization took **13.07 ms** per group at the median. The journal variant cuts datoms by half and payload by only 8%, with similar native apply to the corrected path. Most of its group-time advantage comes before the native turn. The original 4.40× and 6.19× end-to-end ratios cannot be decomposed exactly with this later control.

The compact function's packed path uses **0.45 ms** median per group, growing to **2.78 ms** when it emits the stored journal. The direct arm had the same native datoms and no transaction function. Its 123 ms maximum makes the wide block range a poor estimate of direct-path tail. PREP1's 89 ms prepared guard ran exact native predecessor and identity checks for the incumbent contract. Compact reduces the test to the command, account and exact subject head. The owner-local interval proof uses that head, and fresh assertion IDs use the assigned account position and ordinal. Commands whose invariants span owners still require wider checks.

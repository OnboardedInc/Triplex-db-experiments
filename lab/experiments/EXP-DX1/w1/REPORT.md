**Load input.** Anonymized production totals were 525,208 saves over 29.8 hours, 415 per active person and 19 per subtask, mostly one to three facts. Per-account active-minute median/p99/peak were 3.5/23.5/39.4 saves/s.
**Decision point.** Priority 6 allows 1.25× quiet p99. Its best case at saturation is limited by the unfinished native batch. With saved 30.40 ms alone p99 and an independent random arrival, a 5.71 ms active cycle alone gives 1.10× and a 32 ms batch gives 1.58×. Fair turns still have other ready accounts ahead. At 175/s ungrouped capacity, the optimistic short-tail model first exceeds 1.25× at 90–100 offered saves/s. Full saved tails can break it below 50/s. Use about 90/s as a provisional split threshold for isolation, and treat sustained rates above actual grouped capacity as a separate reason to split. A universal rate guarantee needs the server's service distribution.
**Queue options.** `TX2_LIVE_PRIORITY=1` rotates verified accounts within the live and bulk classes. The SDK can label live, import, activation or notice. Unclaimed work defaults to import. Shared subjects, IDs, claims, ordered senders and whole-scope preconditions retain their predecessor order. Each bulk frame runs alone. Defaults are 96 new or retired rows including journal rows, 64 KiB on the wire, and at most 64 pending bulk frames in a 256-slot queue. `transactBulkFrames` submits caller-authored atomic frames one at a time after the previous durable reply. Storage stalls can extend a frame's time, and sustained live overload can starve bulk.
**Mac queue mechanism.** A/B/B/A at 8, 32 and 80 rows per bulk frame, with eight bulk and two live offers per wave. All 96 priority bulk frames ran solo. The live saves landed in the first three positions in 12/12 priority waves, versus as late as ninth in control. At width 80, bulk/live wire bytes were roughly 235/7.6 kB per wave. For live saves, median ready-to-native entry was 216→102 ms, native-entry hop 17→3 ms and peer await 313→110 ms. These are small busy-Mac samples.
**Field saves.** `TX2_FIELD_COALESCE=1` combines up to eight already-ready same-subject commands in one turn. An idle queue sends immediately. A forced native backlog checked ten edits across two other accounts, every interior cut, individual feed entries, strict clocks and distinct durable receipts. The paced one-account study ran 42/282/473 offers per arm at 3.5/23.5/39.4 saves/s across A/B/B/A. All 3,188 saves used separate native transactions. Synthetic 3-second person sessions across ten accounts gave 0/2,100, 9/14,100 and 0/23,640 folds at 175/s solo capacity. Raw per-person times were unavailable. With the modeled 1/3/8-second sessions and 150–200/s capacity the widest ranges were 0%, 0–7.86% and 0–0.96%, driven by deep sender backlog.
**Owner question.** If several edits of one field are queued, may an earlier edit disappear from durable history and the feed, and what would its sender receive?

**Replaced rows.** `TX2_PREDECESSOR_LOOKUP=1` resolves the authoritative subject, seeks its exact row and checks the retirement prefix on the same serialized view. Wrong, missing or unbound owners use the canonical lookup. The transaction function reuses a checked datom within that invocation and omits redundant existing-entity upserts after resolving IDs. Older scopes use the original function until reopened. The 350-assertion native gate covered imported IDs, wrong owners, competing replacements, stale cuts, retractions, reuse, grouped reads and the rollout guard added after the Mac measurement.
